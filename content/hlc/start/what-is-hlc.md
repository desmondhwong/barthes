# What is HLC?

HLC stands for **Holographic Linguistic Cosmography**. The name gathers three ambitions: attending to distributed cultural patterns, working through language, and mapping a field of possible interpretations. In the source notes, a language model becomes an instrument for exploring a larger cultural landscape rather than only a conversational partner that supplies isolated answers.

“Holographic” describes the proposal that fragments of culture carry relationships to much larger patterns. A sentence can imply an institution, a genre, a social role, and a history of assumptions. The word is doing conceptual work here; it does not establish that language models operate by optical holography or that every fragment contains recoverable access to the whole. That boundary matters throughout [[foundations/language-and-reality|the relation between language and reality]].

“Linguistic” names the principal medium of the early project. The framework begins with a [[foundations/cultural-hologram|cultural hologram]] wider than text, then asks what happens as cultural material becomes records, training data, and generated language. The later [[extensions/multimodal|multimodal extension]] asks how the same questions change for images, sound, and other representations.

“Cosmography” names an orientation toward mapping. Instead of accepting the first fluent answer, a practitioner compares framings, looks for recurring assumptions, and records relationships among concepts. The map is built from observed outputs and human interpretation. It is not a direct instrument readout of a model’s internal coordinates.

A modest way to try HLC is to choose one question, ask for two explicitly different approaches, and compare what each makes easy or difficult to say. This is the beginning of [[practice/parallax|parallax]], and it already captures much of the practical value without requiring commitment to a comprehensive cosmology.
