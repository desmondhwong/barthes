# The cultural hologram

The **cultural hologram** is HLC’s name for the larger field of human expression and practice: speech, writing, images, rituals, techniques, institutions, habits, and forms of tacit knowledge. The term makes the scope of the project larger than a library of documents. A recipe, the embodied skill of cooking it, and the social occasion around a meal participate in culture in different ways.

This is a conceptual totality rather than a dataset we can collect. No inventory could make every lost conversation, private association, or unrecorded practice available. The source framework sometimes uses expansive language about the whole of culture; a practical reading keeps that whole as a horizon, not as a completed object waiting inside a computer.

A useful distinction follows: the cultural hologram is not the [[foundations/digital-trace|digital trace]]. A practice can exist without a digital representation. When it does acquire a representation, the recording selects an aspect of it. A video of a craft may show a hand movement while missing the pressure, judgment, and years of correction that make the movement work.

The metaphor also draws attention to relationships. A small expression can point beyond itself to shared conventions and historical conditions. That relational richness explains why a short model response may evoke an entire worldview. It does not mean the model has recovered every condition behind the expression.

The missing and poorly represented parts of this field reappear in HLC as [[terrain/voids|voids and dark linguistic matter]]. They are a reason to seek people, practices, and records beyond the model, not an invitation to ask the model to speak on their behalf.
