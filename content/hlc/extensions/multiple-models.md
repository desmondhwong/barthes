# Comparing different instruments

HLC extends the telescope analogy to **multiple models**. Different systems can produce different views of a shared question. This adds a dimension to [[practice/parallax|parallax]], alongside changing the lens or source material.

Hold the question and supplied sources steady. Record each response before synthesizing them. Identify differences in claims, omissions, framing, and confidence. Ask which differences the sources resolve and which expose a value judgment or an ambiguity in the task.

An output difference does not explain why the systems differ. Training, architecture, tuning, instructions, and generation conditions may all matter. Without documentation and controls, the comparison is behavioral evidence rather than a causal account of internal geometry.

Agreement also needs care. Models can share conventions and repeat common errors. Several similar answers are not automatically independent witnesses. [[critique/novelty-and-evidence|Evidence checks]] remain necessary, especially for the most fluent answer.

The source imagines personal and specialized models as narrower instruments. Specialization should not be assumed to guarantee accuracy. The [[foundations/corpus-membrane|corpus membrane]] still matters: what was selected, excluded, and made checkable? A good comparison improves the next question rather than merely producing a winner among instruments.
