# Mandala as a compact format

The HLC notes use **mandala** for a compressed arrangement of words around a central concept. The proposed format suspends the flow of explanatory prose so that associations can be compared at a glance. A center, a ring of nearby terms, and a ring of tensions may reveal which ideas a session keeps grouping together.

For a library inquiry, the center might be “access.” Nearby terms could include hours, language, transport, orientation, assistance, and belonging. A second ring might name tensions: openness and staffing, quiet and sociability, preservation and use. The arrangement is an authored sketch of relations, not a scan of a model’s hidden geometry.

This distinction matters because the source sometimes presents compact symbolic output as direct exposure of a latent field. The observable result is a generated text arrangement. Its usefulness can be tested by whether it helps a reader notice a missing relation or formulate a better question; its shape alone does not establish correspondence with internal model structure.

Use the format briefly within the [[practice/telescope-protocol|telescope protocol]]. Begin with a defined question, create the compact field, then return to prose to explain the strongest connection and the weakest one. [[formats/prose-and-maps|Moving between prose and maps]] is part of the method.

A mandala can invite productive ambiguity, but ambiguity also permits projection. If every term seems connected to every other term, the result may be difficult to challenge. Ask which link should be removed and why. That small act of [[practice/exercises/integration|integration]] makes the sketch more discriminating.
