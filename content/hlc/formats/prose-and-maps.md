# Moving between prose and maps

A map makes relations visible; prose explains what a relation claims. HLC benefits from moving between the two. A compact arrangement can interrupt a familiar narrative, while a paragraph can expose an ambiguity hidden by a line between two words. Neither form is inherently more faithful to the subject.

In a [[formats/mandala|mandala]], proximity might mean similarity, dependency, tension, or a loose association. Unless the relation is explained, the reader must supply it. In prose, connective language can make a weak relation sound inevitable. Each format has a characteristic way of hiding uncertainty.

Try translating one map connection into a sentence with a precise verb. Does the first term cause, enable, constrain, resemble, or merely accompany the second? Then translate the sentence back into a small diagram. If the relation changes during translation, record the change rather than treating the diagram as a neutral illustration.

This guide’s own page graph follows an even simpler rule: a link means that one page points to another for further reading. Spatial closeness is produced by the layout and the link structure. It should not be read as a measured distance between concepts inside a language model. The blog is a navigable editorial map.

The broader lesson belongs to [[foundations/language-and-reality|language and reality]]: every representation makes choices. Use [[practice/parallax|parallax]] between formats to discover those choices, and keep a plain account of the claim you intend to carry forward. A useful representation should make the next act of understanding easier, including the act of disagreeing with it.
