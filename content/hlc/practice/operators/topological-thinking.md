# Following relationships through change

HLC’s **topological operator** invites attention to continuity, boundaries, and changing relations. Instead of asking only which category an object belongs to, ask what changes while a relationship persists. This is a way of organizing conceptual work; unless a formal mathematical model is supplied, it should not be confused with a result in topology.

A public room offers a simple example. Its furnishings, opening hours, users, and rules may change. At what point does it cease to function as a shared meeting place? Different answers reveal different criteria: public ownership, practical access, collective use, or the ability to remain without buying anything. Following those changes exposes the structure behind a familiar label.

This operator is useful when a rigid classification obscures a transition. It can also help identify a boundary worth investigating: between a library and a social service, between participation and unpaid work, or between assistance and dependence. The boundary should be described through cases, not merely drawn as an elegant line.

The approach complements [[practice/operators/composition|composition]], which brings elements together, and [[practice/reindexing|reindexing]], which changes the coordinates of a question. A relation may look discontinuous in one description and gradual in another. That disagreement is material for analysis rather than an automatic reason to prefer the more fluid account.

A compelling picture of flows can simplify away institutions, material constraints, or discrete decisions that matter. The operator earns its place when it clarifies a concrete change that a simpler account missed.
