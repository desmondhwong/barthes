# Choosing a lens

A **lens** directs attention toward certain questions and away from others. HLC borrows the word from instruments, but the practical operation is interpretive: specify a perspective, its concerns, and the kind of evidence it should use. “Analyze this critically” is less informative than asking who benefits, who bears costs, and which alternatives the account treats as unavailable.

Different lenses can be applied to the same object. A library seen through maintenance raises questions about staffing and repair. A historical lens asks how its purposes changed. A phenomenological lens asks how a visitor experiences entry, orientation, and belonging. The point is to state what each lens makes salient rather than pretend that any one lens sees everything.

A lens is also a source of distortion. Asking for a market analysis can make value appear primarily as a price or transaction. Asking for symbolic interpretation can encourage connections that outrun the evidence. A useful response should name these exclusions as well as develop its chosen perspective.

Try two passes with the same source material and different lenses. Then ask which disagreements come from facts, which come from values, and which come from vocabulary. This comparison becomes [[practice/parallax|parallax]] when you use the differences to revise your understanding.

Lens choice belongs to the [[foundations/activation-field|activation field]] and to the reader’s responsibilities. It cannot be outsourced entirely to the model. In particular, a model’s selection of “the most important perspectives” may already reproduce the [[terrain/attractors|attractors]] you hoped to examine. Record the selection and keep a place for a perspective that the first pass omitted.
