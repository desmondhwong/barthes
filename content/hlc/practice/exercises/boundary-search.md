# Exercise: search a boundary

A **boundary search** places two domains beside each other and asks for a useful contact point. The HLC telescope notes propose it as a way to move beyond a familiar framing without drifting into arbitrary association. The practical standard is coherence: the connection must preserve enough of both domains to illuminate a specific question.

Take library access and maintenance practice. A first connection might be that access depends on recurring care rather than only on a one-time design decision. That observation becomes more precise when applied to broken lifts, outdated instructions, missing translations, or staff availability. The connection earns value by changing what someone would inspect.

## Make the bridge explicit

Ask for three candidate connections. For each, request the assumption borrowed from the first domain, the assumption borrowed from the second, a concrete example, and a place where the analogy fails. A candidate that cannot name its failure condition may be too vague to use.

Then choose the least extravagant candidate that changes a real question. You do not need the most unusual phrase. A clear distinction such as “access as recurring maintenance” may support better inquiry than a grand invented ontology.

It should also return to the [[practice/exercises/attractor-survey|attractor survey]]: perhaps the new idea is already a familiar one in another discipline. That discovery is useful, even though it weakens a claim to novelty.

Finish with [[practice/exercises/integration|integration]]. Preserve the example, the failed analogy, and a question that could be checked outside the conversation. Those details turn a suggestive bridge into something another reader can examine.
