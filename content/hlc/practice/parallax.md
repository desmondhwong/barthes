# Parallax through comparison

In HLC, **parallax** means learning from the differences between views. The astronomical analogy suggests that a change in position can reveal structure. In a language-model session, the corresponding move is to vary a lens, question, source packet, or model, then examine what remains stable and what moves.

Suppose two accounts of a library agree that longer opening hours matter. One treats access as consumer convenience; another treats it as a condition for participation in public life. The shared proposal does not erase the different justifications. Those justifications may lead to different decisions when money, staffing, or eligibility becomes contested.

The comparison can proceed in three steps: describe the agreement, identify the assumption behind each difference, and find a case that would make the difference consequential. The final step prevents parallax from becoming a collection of perspectives with no bearing on the question.

Agreement is not independent confirmation merely because it appears in several outputs. Prompts can share assumptions, and [[extensions/multiple-models|different models]] can draw on overlapping conventions and sources. Likewise, disagreement can reflect an error rather than a profound difference in worldview. Each needs examination.

Parallax is therefore a method for generating and refining questions, not an automatic truth detector. It works alongside [[critique/novelty-and-evidence|evidence checks]] and [[practice/reindexing|reindexing]]. When a comparison changes the terms of the inquiry, write down the changed question. That small record is often a more durable result than the several pages of commentary that produced it.
